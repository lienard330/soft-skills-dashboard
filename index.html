60.1% is well above the 50% chance baseline for a two-class problem, though lower than the 90%+ accuracy reported in prior machine-learning studies on this same dataset. Those studies used tuned ensemble models (Random Forest, SVM, gradient boosting); this study deliberately uses a single transparent logistic regression instead, to explain which soft skills relate to employability rather than to maximize raw prediction accuracy.

Why some bars point the "wrong" way: the seven soft-skill ratings are highly correlated with one another (e.g. Manner of Speaking and Ability to Present Ideas correlate at r = .73). Once the model accounts for that overlap, a factor's own weight can come out negative even though it correlates positively with employability on its own — that's a known statistical side-effect of correlated predictors (multicollinearity), not an error.
